Methodology Deep Dive

Problem Analysis

Through careful measurement on the full training labels, we uncovered several distinct patterns within the generator process:

  • One owner per record: Each Source 2/3 record matches at most one Source 1 record, and 26% match none.
  • Indic scripts: 18% of India Source 2/3 names are in Indic scripts. We discovered they act as a word-level dictionary with ~1,350 words, allowing for accurate transliteration via positional alignment.
  • Noise vs. Siblings: We distinguished "true copies" which suffer from noise (case changes, typos, website forms) versus "siblings"—near-copies of a Source 1 business that are actually different businesses (e.g. same name on the same street but a different legal form).
  • France (test only): Following the sibling mechanism, French records utilized French words and legal forms. As France lacked training labels, we needed an adaptive approach.

Solution Strategy

Our approach revolves around a two-stage gradient boosting architecture (Blocking + Classifier) employing many-to-one assignments.

Core Innovations

1. Retrieval: Using joint multi-channel scoring rather than capped per-Source-1 blocks.
2. Generator Targeting: Features specifically built to tackle the generator's sibling mechanism, such as cross-fitted token/legal-form risk and label-free house-number stats.
3. Unseen-Country Proxy: A leave-one-country-out model validated by training on US and scoring India without labels.
4. Label-Free Diagnosis: Self-training and noise-word rule measurement for French records.

Candidate Generation (Blocking)

Candidate pairs are retrieved via an inverted index per country. Three channels are used:

  1. Core-name word unigrams and adjacent bigrams
  2. Character 4-grams of the joined core name (catches typos and domains)
  3. Address tokens and numbers

A stage-0 LightGBM re-ranker heavily prunes candidates, sending only 8.3M test pairs to the final stage, without losing true matches (recall of 98.86%).

Matching Model

We use LightGBM models with 2-fold cross-fitting by Source 1 entity. Stage 1 uses 82 features (name ratios, token risks, legal-form conflicts, address stats), while stage 2 adds 9 competition-context features.

For robustness, five US/India stage-1/stage-2 chains are averaged. The expected-F0.5 rule determines how many assigned candidates to keep per Source 1 entity.
